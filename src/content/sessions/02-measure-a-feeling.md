## Preparation

Read the week 2 lecture and bring your five-jump table from its task. If you
didn't finish it, bring what you have — the session starts from wherever
your table actually is, not from a finished one.

## Activity

1. In pairs, both run the same immediate-mode preset for five jumps each, and
   compare tables side by side. They will not match exactly — human hold
   times vary — but the trend (longer hold, taller jump, longer airtime)
   should be visible in both.
2. Add the predicted-airtime column from the lecture task if you haven't
   already, and check it against the measured column together. Where a pair
   disagrees on the prediction method, work out why before moving on.
3. As a group: name one source of imprecision in the measurement itself
   (reaction time before a press registers, frame timing, rounding in the
   displayed number) versus one source of imprecision in the *jump* (the
   hold itself varying between attempts). These are different problems, and
   the Jump Autopsy assessment in week 3 asks you to acknowledge both kinds.

## Output

The completed six-column table and the two sentences from the spec above,
handed to your tutor at the end of the session.

## Criteria

Not marked, but treated as the direct rehearsal for Jump Autopsy's method
section: if you can't yet state how many trials you ran and under what
preset clearly enough for a partner to reproduce your table, that's the gap
to close before week 3.
