## Preparation

Read the week 1 lecture and open the [home page](/) once before the session,
just to see that it loads and a jump runs. Nothing to install, nothing to
configure — if it doesn't run, say so at the start of the session rather than
during it.

## Activity

Working alone or in pairs, at a laptop each:

1. Run at least three jumps: one short press in immediate mode, one full hold
   in immediate mode, and one charged jump. Fill in the table from the
   lecture task as you go — don't reconstruct it from memory afterward.
2. Compare your table with the pair next to you. Everyone's numbers should
   differ (nobody presses identically), but the *pattern* — hold longer, jump
   higher — should hold for both tables. If it doesn't, that's worth raising.
3. As a group: name one moment in a jump that happens before the character
   leaves the ground, in a game you know. It doesn't have to be dramatic —
   a controller has to register the input before anything moves, and that
   gap is real even when nothing is drawn for it.

## Output

The completed table and the two sentences from the spec above, handed to your
tutor at the end of the session or brought to next week's workshop.

## Criteria

The table's numbers come from the home trial, not a guess dressed up as one
— if two rows report identical numbers to three decimal places, that's a
sign they were copied rather than measured. Beyond that, this session isn't
marked: it's the rehearsal for the [Jump Autopsy](/assessments/jump-autopsy/)
assessment in week 3, which asks for the same kind of table at a larger scale.
