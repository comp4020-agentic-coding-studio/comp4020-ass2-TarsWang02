## Question

Two jumps reach the same apex height. In one, gravity is low and launch speed
is low. In the other, gravity is high and launch speed is high. Are these the
same jump wearing different numbers, or do they feel different — and if they
feel different, what number would you look at to prove it?

## Prediction (skip if you'd rather just look)

Before running anything: do you expect the *time in the air* to be the same
for both jumps in the question above, given they reach the same height?

## Experiment

Open the [Jump Lab](/jump-lab/), pick the Floaty preset (low gravity, low
launch speed) and the Precise preset (higher gravity, higher launch speed),
and run three immediate-mode trials of each. Log height and airtime for
both. You'll likely find their heights land in a similar range but their
airtimes don't — low gravity keeps you up longer for the same height,
because gravity is what pulls you back down, and launch speed only decides
how fast you left the ground.

## Explanation

Height comes from the balance of both numbers together — how fast you leave,
against how hard you're pulled back — so two very different pairs of values
can produce a similar peak. Airtime, though, is set mostly by gravity alone:
weaker gravity means a slower fall, which means more time to react, aim, and
correct once you're already committed to the jump. That's the whole
difference between "floaty" and "precise" as *feelings*, not just as labels
on a preset button.

<details>
<summary>Why the Gravity Lab locks in your settings at takeoff, not at landing</summary>

Every trial's logged row shows the gravity and launch speed values from the
moment you left the ground, even if you kept dragging sliders while airborne.
If a mid-air edit changed the numbers under a jump already in progress, the
same input could land in two different places depending on when you nudged a
slider — which would make every trial before this lecture's "reproducible
method" pointless. Parameter changes only ever apply to the *next* trial.

</details>

## Task

Using the Gravity Lab, hold launch speed fixed and run three trials each at
two different gravity values of your choosing. Report height and airtime for
both settings, and one sentence on which of the two numbers moved more.
