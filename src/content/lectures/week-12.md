## Question

You now have three presets, a reproducible measurement method, a
single-variable comparison tool, and at least one observation of a real
person playing something you built. If someone asked you "which jump feels
best," what would you actually be able to say — and what could you still
not honestly claim?

## Prediction (skip if you'd rather just look)

Before writing anything: can distance jumped, or whether a gap was cleared,
settle an argument about which of two jumps feels better? If not, what
would have to be true of a claim for it to actually settle that argument?

## Experiment

Look back at your week 3 measurement table and your week 10 A/B comparison
side by side. The first proves you can measure one configuration
reproducibly; the second proves you can isolate the effect of one changed
parameter. Neither one, by itself, tells you anything about whether a human
enjoyed either result — that's what week 11's playtest note was for. Put
together, they're three separate kinds of evidence, and none of them
substitutes for either of the others.

## Explanation

This course split into four phases for a reason: **measure** established
that a number is only evidence if someone else could reproduce it;
**control** showed that gravity, launch speed, air control and the
forgiveness windows are separable levers, each doing one job; **express**
showed that posture is a completely different kind of lever, one that never
touches the physics at all; **evaluate** showed that a fair comparison
changes one thing at a time, and that a real observation of another person
playing outweighs any number of your own confident guesses. A convincing
final claim about a jump's feel draws on all four — a measurement table, an
isolated comparison, and a real playtest observation — and is explicit about
which parts are measured fact and which are your design judgement.

<details>
<summary>Why "distance jumped" was never going to be the answer</summary>

Two jumps of an identical distance can differ in every property this course
has spent twelve weeks distinguishing: airtime, forgiveness, redirect-
ability, how the landing reads, whether a real player found it satisfying
or fought with it. Collapsing all of that into a single distance number
throws away everything the last eleven weeks measured.

</details>
