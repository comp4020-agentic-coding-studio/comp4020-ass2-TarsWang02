## The brief

> One character. Three personalities. You decide which one is right — and
> show your working.

This is the same shared jump engine every week has used, at full stretch. The
[Jump Lab](/jump-lab/) starts you with Floaty, Heavy and Precise; you're
free to use them as given or as a starting point for your own tuning, as
long as the final personalities are clearly distinguishable from each other
by more than a slider label.

The comparison at the centre of this brief is deliberately narrow: change
**one** parameter between your A and B trials, not several at once, and
replay them with the Lab's A/B tool so the difference is something a marker
can watch rather than take your word for. A wide, unfocused comparison
("I changed gravity, air control and pose together") makes it impossible to
say which change caused what — that's the entire reason week 10's fair-test
discipline exists.

The play-test is not optional and not simulated. Hand the Lab to someone
else — a classmate, a housemate, anyone who hasn't tuned these presets
themselves — and watch them try the jump without coaching them through it.
Write down what actually happened, even if it's mundane. "They jumped the
gap on the first try and stopped paying attention to the character" is a
usable observation; a problem you expected but didn't observe is not.

## What you submit

1. **Preset comparison table** — Floaty, Heavy, Precise, each with several
   real trials and their measurements.
2. **One single-variable A/B comparison** — a saved replay link, the one
   parameter you changed, and what you expected versus what the replay
   showed.
3. **Play-test account** — who played, what you asked them to do (or
   deliberately didn't explain), and the specific problem they hit.
4. **Revision** — the setting you changed in response, and a before/after
   trial demonstrating it.
5. **Final explanation** (roughly 400–600 words) — the feel you were
   designing for, and why your data supports it. Distance jumped and whether
   the gap was cleared are not, by themselves, evidence of quality: a jump
   that clears every gap by pure air-control correction and a jump that
   clears none because it's too heavy are both measurable, and neither is
   automatically "better" without you saying what you were optimising for.
