# Same Path, Different Body

Week 7 — The Anatomy of a Jump

---

## Question

Two characters follow the exact same recorded trajectory through the air.

Same height. Same displacement. Same airtime, frame for frame.

Can they still look like they're jumping differently?

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Yes — and nothing about the path has to change

What's left to change, once the path is fixed?

---

## What the physics engine owns

- gravity, launch speed → how high, how long
- air control → how much a held direction redirects you mid-air
- release cut, coyote time, jump buffer → forgiveness around timing

None of this is about how the body *looks*.

---

## What pose owns

- arm swing
- leg tuck
- landing compression

Computed **from** the trajectory, after it's already decided. Never fed back
into it.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A pose that changed the outcome wouldn't be a pose. It would be a hidden
> physics parameter wearing a costume.

---

## The comparison

Load the identical trajectory into two bodies.

Push one body's arm swing and landing compression high. Push the other's
low. Change nothing about the path.

One reads as loose and reactive. One reads as stiff and controlled.

The overlay of their paths is a single line.

---

## Why this had to be true earlier, not just today

If pose leaked into collision, every gravity comparison since week 4 would
secretly have been a gravity-*and*-posture comparison.

The separation isn't this week's lesson. It's a constraint the whole course
has depended on since week 4.

---

## Across the three presets

| Preset  | Physics feel                  | A pose that could match it       |
| ------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| Floaty  | slow fall, easy mid-air correction | loose arm swing, soft landing |
| Heavy   | fast fall, committed direction | tucked limbs, hard compression    |
| Precise | moderate fall, locked direction | minimal swing, controlled settle |

These pairings are a starting point, not a rule — the posture comparison
tool exists so you can test your own.
