# Policies and support

These rules apply across all three assessments — Jump Autopsy, Take Control,
and One Body, Three Personalities — so each brief can link here instead of
restating them.

## Access

Every core task in this course has a non-coding route: the Jump Lab's
sliders, presets, trial log, "Copy configuration" link, and A/B comparison
are all built to be operated without writing a line of code, and every
assessment can be completed entirely through them. Optional, guided
starter-code modifications appear later in the course for students who want
them, but no assessment criterion requires code, and none rewards code
complexity for its own sake. If a control, label, or piece of feedback is
not usable with a keyboard, not readable at your device's default zoom, or
depends on sound to convey information no visual channel also carries,
that's a bug in the platform, not a gap in your submission — report it to
your tutor and you will not be penalised for a measurement you couldn't
take because of it.

## Workload

Each session is designed to rehearse, at low stakes, exactly the format its
nearest assessment is marked on — attending sessions is the most direct
preparation available for the assessment attached to that phase of the
course. Session outputs are described in each session page's spec but are
not marked; they exist so the first time you produce a piece of required
evidence isn't also the first time it's due. Lecture tasks are short enough
to complete before the following session and are the input that session's
activities build on.

## Evidence

Every number in a submission must trace back to an actual trial run in the
Jump Lab — a value you remember, estimate, or reconstruct from a screenshot
taken before you started editing is not acceptable evidence, even if it's
correct. Where a brief asks for a reproducible method, that means naming the
exact configuration (preset name, or a "Copy configuration" link, or both),
the mode, and the number of trials, in enough detail that a marker could run
the same trials and land within your reported range. Where a brief asks for
a playtest observation, that means a concrete, written account of what a
real person did, not a general impression of how the session went.

## Attribution

Work submitted for this course must be your own measurements, your own
written explanations, and your own observations of anyone you playtested
with — but the playtesting itself is expected to involve another person,
and you should name who you tested with (a first name or initials is
enough) in the relevant section of your submission. If you discussed an
approach with a classmate before running your own trials, say so briefly;
it does not cost you marks and keeps the record honest. Do not present a
classmate's trial data, configuration link, or written observation as your
own.

## Submission

Submit through the same channel for all three assessments, as a single
document containing your tables, links and written responses in the order
each brief lists its criteria — this makes marking against those criteria
straightforward and is the fastest way to make sure nothing you produced
gets missed. Configuration links and any other URLs should be pasted in
full, not shortened, so they can be checked directly. Late submissions and
extension requests are handled individually by your tutor; raise them as
early as you can, ideally before the deadline rather than after it.
